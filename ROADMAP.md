# SUPER CHILLED STUDIO

# ROADMAP

> A roadmap predicts destinations.
>
> A chart preserves bearings.
>
> The Studio follows the latter.

---

# Purpose

This document describes the long-term direction of Forge and Super Chilled Studio.

It is not a schedule, a promise or a list of tasks.

It gives future contributors a shared bearing when the path ahead is unclear.

Protects creative intent from technical momentum.

Forge should grow only when that growth helps the Studio create places worth returning to.

---

# The Direction of Travel

The Studio begins with canon.

Canon becomes trustworthy representation.

Trustworthy representation protects continuity.

Continuity supports craftsmanship.

Craftsmanship allows places to live.

Forge first learns.

Then it remembers.

Then it helps.

Never in the opposite order.

The direction is not toward maximum automation.

It is toward deeper understanding, stronger memory and quieter support.

---

# The Measure of Progress

Progress is recognised in deeper places.

A world should become more believable, more lived in and more capable of rewarding a return visit.

Progress is recognised in stronger continuity.

Objects age instead of resetting. Beacons remain. The Bonsai grows. Life leaves evidence.

Progress is recognised in quieter craftsmanship.

Repetition recedes so that attention can return to atmosphere, judgement and the small decisions that make a place feel real.

Progress is recognised in greater trust.

Canon becomes easier to rely on. Contributors understand what belongs, what must remain and where creative uncertainty still needs a human decision.

Progress is recognised in better support for human judgement.

Forge should clarify choices without making creative decisions on behalf of the people responsible for the Studio.

Progress is recognised when viewers want to return.

Not because something new demands their attention, but because the place remembers them.

---

# Where We Stand

The Studio has a constitution.

Its mission, philosophy and laws establish what every future capability must protect.

The five worlds form one continuous life journey. Each has a stable identity, a purpose, an emotional core, a Beacon, a relationship to the Bonsai, a soundscape, evidence of time and elements that cannot be casually changed.

The visual language of the Studio trusts the viewer. It favours evidence over explanation, wear over novelty, negative space over clutter and quiet discovery over spectacle.

Recurring objects carry identity, memory and continuity. Their meaning grows through presence, repair, age and return.

Forge can hold the worlds and their recurring objects as dependable canon. It can find them by stable identity, preserve their wording and relationships, and present them clearly to the people caring for the Studio.

The foundation is no longer only an idea.

It is something the Studio can stand on.

---

# The Next Horizon

The next horizon is trust made practical.

Worlds, objects and their relationships should become equally easy to understand without separating them from the human-readable canon that gives them meaning.

Continuity conflicts should become visible before they reach a place. A contributor should be able to understand what has appeared, what has changed, what is permanent and what remains uncertain.

Forge should help answer the quiet questions that protect coherence:

What must remain?

What has earned its wear?

What belongs to this resident?

What has already told this story more honestly than something new could?

The next horizon is reached when contributors can move through canon with confidence and return to craftsmanship without carrying the entire Studio in memory.

---

# The Distant Horizon

In the distance, Forge becomes the Studio's long memory.

It remembers continuity across every return to a world.

Technology changes without erasing personal history. Objects accumulate meaning without becoming clutter. Beacons age naturally without losing their identity. The protagonist's Bonsai continues its quiet journey across decades.

Sound, light, weather and motion remain grounded in each place. Production support understands the fixed camera, the noise budget, recognition levels and the removal test before it offers assistance.

Creative decisions leave understandable traces. Future contributors can discover not only what was decided, but what that decision protects.

Repetitive technical work becomes quieter. Human approval remains deliberate. The World Keeper remains responsible for canon.

The distant horizon is not autonomous production.

It is a dependable Studio engine that remembers, protects and assists while the worlds continue to belong to people.

---

# The World Keeper

The Studio is inherited.

Every future contributor receives places shaped by decisions, restraint and care that came before them.

Their responsibility is not to make the Studio larger.

Their responsibility is to leave it more coherent than they found it.

Growth sometimes means adding.

Sometimes refusing.

Sometimes removing.

The Studio grows through stewardship rather than accumulation.

The World Keeper protects the meaning of the whole. Every contributor shares in that stewardship whenever they preserve continuity, question unnecessary novelty, repair an inconsistency or choose silence over noise.

---

# Never Build

The Studio will never become a content-volume engine.

It will never pursue novelty merely to appear active.

It will never move the canonical camera, place objects without purpose or animate something simply because motion is available.

It will never replace evidence with exposition, readable meaning with generated gibberish or lived-in history with decorative clutter.

It will never silently change canon, remove a Beacon, confuse the Coffee Café house Bonsai with the protagonist's Bonsai or reset continuity between visits.

It will never allow engagement metrics to determine atmosphere, pacing or emotional meaning.

It will never allow automation to overrule the World Keeper or remove deliberate human approval from a creative decision.

It will never mistake technical possibility for creative necessity.

These are not limitations waiting to be overcome.

They are the shoreline that gives the Studio its shape.

---

# The Test for Every Step

Does this help create a place worth returning to?

Does it protect canon and continuity?

Does it preserve human creative judgement?

Does it remove repetition without removing care?

Does it make the Studio easier for the next contributor to understand and trust?

Would the place lose part of its story if this disappeared?

If the answer is unclear, the step does not yet belong.

---

# Guiding Principle

The Studio does not become great by becoming larger.

It becomes great by remembering.

Every decision should leave the next contributor with more trust than uncertainty.

Leave every place—and every contributor—a little better than you found them.

The Studio is never finished.

Only cared for.
