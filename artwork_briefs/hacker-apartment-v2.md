# SUPER CHILLED STUDIO

# Production Brief

## World

The Hacker Apartment

---

## Purpose

Render the canonical Hacker Apartment.

This is not concept art.

It is a believable private sanctuary that has quietly evolved over years of late-night building, learning and persistence.

The goal is not to depict a hacker.

The goal is to make the viewer wish they lived here.

---

## Viewer Experience

The viewer is the resident.

The chair remains empty because the viewer occupies it.

The room should feel as though someone has just stepped away for a few minutes and will return to continue building.

Nothing should feel staged for the camera.

---

## Composition

The camera remains completely stationary.

Eye-level perspective.

Natural human field of view.

The desk is the visual centre of the room.

Negative space is intentional.

The apartment is home.

The city is context.

The outside world should support the room rather than compete with it.

---

## Environment

A compact Neo-Tokyo apartment during a rainy evening.

The room is modest rather than luxurious.

Everything has earned its place over years of use.

Nothing feels newly purchased without reason.

The apartment reflects someone who invests in their work rather than decoration.

---

## Desk

The desk feels actively used.

Objects have clear purpose.

There is evidence of ongoing work.

The workspace feels organised by habit rather than perfection.

Small imperfections should naturally accumulate.

---

## Monitor

The monitor displays believable work.

It must never display:

- inspirational slogans
- decorative typography
- cinematic hacker interfaces
- meaningless streams of code
- AI-generated gibberish

The display should resemble a real project someone genuinely works on.

---

## Bonsai

The protagonist's Bonsai is clearly recognisable as a Bonsai tree.

It must never resemble:

- a cactus
- a succulent
- a generic houseplant

Characteristics include:

- visible woody trunk
- trained branching
- miniature tree silhouette
- healthy but imperfect growth

It quietly communicates patience and continuity.

---

## Lighting

Lighting comes from believable practical sources.

Warm interior lighting.

Cool rainy city beyond the window.

The room should feel comfortable rather than dramatic.

Nothing glows simply because it looks impressive.

---

## The City

Neo-Tokyo exists outside.

Its purpose is to remind the viewer that life continues beyond the apartment.

Neon belongs primarily to the city.

The apartment should never feel overwhelmed by outside lighting.

The city supports the room.

It never dominates it.

---

## Materials

Materials should appear authentic.

Wood looks like wood.

Fabric looks used.

Metal shows age.

Plastic softens over time.

Glass carries rain naturally.

Nothing feels sterile.

Nothing feels like concept art.

---

## Wear

The room has history.

Objects show repair.

Furniture softens.

Edges wear naturally.

Technology evolves gradually rather than all at once.

Everything suggests years of quiet persistence.

---

## Objects

Every object earns its place.

Objects communicate through evidence rather than explanation.

Identity emerges from accumulated routines, repairs and personal history.

If removing an object would not reduce the room's story, it should not exist.

---

## Things To Avoid

Avoid:

- motivational posters
- inspirational quotes
- decorative hacker aesthetics
- excessive RGB lighting
- fake code
- fake newspapers
- fake books
- unreadable AI text
- unnecessary clutter
- dramatic camera angles
- exaggerated cyberpunk styling
- visual noise

The room should never try to impress the viewer.

It should simply exist.

---

## Success Criteria

The finished artwork succeeds when:

- the viewer immediately feels welcome
- the room feels genuinely lived in
- the Bonsai is unmistakably a Bonsai
- the desk remains the visual centre
- the monitor resembles real work
- every object appears intentionally chosen
- the city enhances rather than distracts
- environmental storytelling replaces explanation
- the room feels like someone just stepped away
- the viewer wishes they lived there

If two objects compete for attention, remove one.

If one object explains what another already implies, remove it.

Trust the viewer.

The room tells the story quietly.
