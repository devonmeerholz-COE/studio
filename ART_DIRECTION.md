# SUPER CHILLED STUDIO
# Art Direction Bible

Version 1.0

---

> **We design memories, not images.**

Every artistic decision should strengthen the feeling of having visited a real place.

If an element exists only to impress the viewer, remove it.

If it helps the viewer remember the place months later, keep it.

---

# Purpose

We do not create beautiful images.

We create believable places.

Every world should feel like it existed long before the viewer arrived and will continue to exist after they leave.

The viewer is not observing a set.

They are quietly visiting someone's life.

---

# The Viewer

We trust the viewer.

The viewer is intelligent.

The viewer notices details.

The viewer enjoys discovery.

We never explain what they can naturally infer.

Every return visit should reveal something previously unnoticed.

Our goal is not to impress the viewer.

Our goal is to make them feel like they have been there before.

---

# Environmental Storytelling

The room tells the story.

The resident never speaks.

The room never explains itself.

History is communicated through evidence.

Never exposition.

Every meaningful object must answer one question:

**"Why is this here?"**

If it cannot answer that question, it does not belong.

---

# Evidence Over Explanation

Whenever there are two possible ways to communicate an idea:

❌ Explain it.

✅ Show evidence of it.

Examples:

Instead of a motivational sign...

Show a repaired keyboard.

Instead of a quote about persistence...

Show years of wear.

Instead of telling the viewer someone works hard...

Leave unfinished work on the desk.

Objects communicate more honestly than words.

---

# Text

Readable text is rare.

Readable text should exist only when a real resident would naturally own it.

Examples:

✓ software interfaces

✓ notebook covers

✓ coffee mugs

✓ handwritten notes

✓ meaningful book titles

Avoid:

✗ motivational slogans

✗ inspirational posters

✗ decorative typography

✗ text that explains the room

✗ AI-generated gibberish

If an object communicates the same idea...

Remove the text.

Unreadable AI text immediately breaks immersion and should never become canon.

---

# Objects

Objects carry narrative.

Decoration does not.

Every object belongs to one of five categories.

## Canon

Permanent living symbols of the Studio.

Examples:

- protagonist’s Bonsai
- Coffee Café house Bonsai

These are never removed from their established continuity.

---

## Beacon

Permanent world-specific anchors that tell returning viewers they are home.

Examples:

- library desk lamp
- coffee pot
- closed laptop bag
- Earth

Their identity and emotional purpose remain constant.

---

## Identity

Objects that quietly reveal the resident’s interests, routines, and personality.

Examples:

- evolving backpack
- mech figure and mementos
- personal keepsakes

Identity objects reveal character without explaining it.

They should reward observation rather than attract attention.

---

## Tool

Objects the resident actively uses to learn, work, create, or listen.

Examples:

- laptop
- keyboard
- notebook
- headphones
- favourite tools

Tools reveal the resident through use, wear, repair, and continued purpose.

Nothing should feel newly purchased without a reason.

---

## Memory

Objects that preserve emotional connections across time.

Examples:

- red panda plush
- tape deck
- diner mug
- keepsakes from Earth

These should never draw attention.

They gain meaning through familiarity, wear, and return.

---

# Imperfection

Real places are imperfect.

Nothing should feel manufactured for the camera.

Wear is beautiful.

Repair is beautiful.

Patina is beautiful.

Asymmetry is beautiful.

Technology is replaced slowly.

Furniture develops history.

Objects accumulate naturally.

The Studio should always feel lived in rather than decorated.

---

# Visual Hierarchy

Every room has one primary focal point.

Everything else supports it.

If multiple objects compete equally for attention...

Remove one.

Negative space is valuable.

Quietness is valuable.

Restraint is valuable.

The eye should never feel overwhelmed.

---

# Lighting

Light guides attention.

It never performs.

Warm and cool lighting should coexist naturally.

Nothing should pulse dramatically.

Nothing should flash.

Nothing should exist purely because it looks impressive.

Light reveals.

It never demands attention.

---

# Colour

Every world owns its own palette.

Accent colours exist only to support emotion.

Never introduce colour simply because it looks exciting.

Neon belongs to the city first.

The room second.

---

# Camera

The camera is an observer.

It never performs.

No dramatic framing.

No cinematic tricks.

The room earns the viewer's attention.

Not the camera.

---

# Motion

Movement must feel inevitable.

Rain falls.

Steam rises.

Monitors breathe softly.

Plants respond gently.

Nothing moves because animation is available.

Movement exists only where life naturally creates it.

---

# The Resident

The resident is never present.

The viewer becomes the resident.

The empty chair is occupied by imagination.

The room should feel as though someone has just stepped away.

---

# The Removal Test

Every object must survive this question:

"If this disappeared tomorrow, would the room lose part of its story?"

If the answer is no...

Remove it.

Every object must earn its place.

---

# The Noise Budget

Attention is finite.

Every bright colour...

Every light...

Every sign...

Every object...

Every piece of readable text...

Consumes attention.

When in doubt:

Remove something.

Never add something.

---

# The Discovery Test

The first visit should create comfort.

The second visit should reveal history.

The tenth visit should reveal something never noticed before.

The Studio rewards observation.

It never rewards speed.

---

# Restraint

When uncertain...

remove something.

Silence is stronger than noise.

Negative space is stronger than clutter.

Suggestion is stronger than explanation.

Trust is stronger than instruction.

The viewer completes the story.

---

# The Final Question

Before approving any artwork ask:

**"Could this room exist without us?"**

If the answer is yes...

Keep going.

If the answer is no...

Simplify.

---

# The Final Rule

The room should never try to impress the viewer.

It should simply exist.

The viewer will do the rest.
